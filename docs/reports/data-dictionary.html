Data Dictionary

Sources, variables, units, coverage, and transformations

This study draws on tourism, employment, accommodation, economic, and national park records to examine change in the U.S. Virgin Islands from 2000 through 2025. Sources differ in coverage, frequency, definitions, and reporting practices; the measures are not always directly comparable.

Raw source files are stored in data/raw/. Source URLs, local file paths, coverage, and acquisition notes are catalogued in src/acquisition.py. Validation findings and documented data-quality decisions are maintained in src/validation.py.

Source-specific qualifications

Visitor statistics. USVIBER publications do not always report internally consistent totals. Published total visitors, excursionists, and air visitors are retained as separate measures rather than reconstructed from components that do not reliably sum to the published totals. Historical reports also revise some earlier years. The analysis uses a documented combination of report vintages; figures near the transition between vintages should be interpreted with care.

Accommodation data. USVIBER inventory surveys include a broader range of accommodation providers than Census payroll-based establishment counts. These are different measures, not competing estimates of an identical population. Census guestroom counts also require a classification crosswalk: the 2007 and 2012 figures use the A10 industry total, while the 2017 and 2022 figures use NAPCS product code 7003825000. The 2017 count is a December 31 snapshot, after Hurricanes Irma and Maria.

The hotel-report extraction contains 396 source observations from 66 PDFs. The selected annual series is retained separately from the source observations and conflict log. Available room nights begin in 2007; occupied room nights are missing for 2002; reported occupancy is missing for 2003 and 2018. The 2025 occupied-night and occupancy figures cover only part of the year.

Employment. The employment sources describe different populations and reference periods. FRED Leisure and Hospitality includes Arts, Entertainment, and Recreation as well as Accommodation and Food Services. QCEW covers UI-reported jobs in specified industries. Census employment is a March snapshot, while USVIBER tourism employment uses its own category definitions. These series provide context for one another but should not be treated as interchangeable.

Prices and compensation. Mainland CPI-U is used as a proxy to express QCEW wage measures in approximate 2016 dollars. It is not a USVI-specific cost-of-living measure. The available USVI CPI series ends in 2021 and contains a substantial 2018 discontinuity that has not been resolved; it is therefore not used as the deflator.

Economic output. BEA GDP and industry value added have different end years. Accommodation and Food Services value added includes activity beyond visitor spending and excludes tourism-related activity recorded in other industries. Its share of GDP is therefore an approximate measure of the industry’s contribution, not a tourism-GDP estimate.

National park visitation. VIIS visitation estimates from 2003–2021 and 2022–2025 were produced under different counting methods. The later series is treated as a distinct methodological period. Data from 2026 are excluded because the park shifted to a different traffic-counter-based approach. Park visits should not be interpreted as a count of individual visitors.

Data availability and reproducibility

The project preserves source files separately from derived datasets. Acquisition metadata are recorded in src/acquisition.py; data loading and preparation are handled in src/cleaning.py. Hotel-report observations, selected values, and conflicts are retained in separate extraction outputs so that the annual series can be traced back to the underlying reports.

Missing observations are not interpolated. Partial-year figures, changes in source definitions, and unresolved discrepancies are identified rather than silently treated as continuous annual measurements.
