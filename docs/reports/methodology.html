Sources & Methodology

Citations, analytical choices, crosswalks, and known limitations

Research Design

This study examines the relationship between tourism, accommodation capacity, employment, and economic activity in the U.S. Virgin Islands from 2000 through 2025. It uses descriptive time-series analysis and cross-source comparisons to assess the scale and unevenness of the territory’s tourism recovery following Hurricanes Irma and Maria (2017) and the COVID-19 pandemic (2020).

The analysis combines visitor statistics, accommodation surveys, federal employment and economic data, business census snapshots, and National Park Service visitation records. These sources measure different aspects of the tourism economy and are not treated as interchangeable.

The study is descriptive. It identifies changes and divergences across indicators but does not estimate the independent causal effects of hurricanes, the pandemic, or federal recovery spending.

Data Acquisition and Preparation

Original source files are retained in data/raw/. Source metadata, including URLs, local paths, coverage, frequency, and known limitations, are maintained in src/acquisition.py. Processing and loading functions are maintained in src/cleaning.py; chart construction is centralized in src/charts.py.

Manually extracted hotel statistics from USVIBER reports are stored as separate annual source files and combined by src/hotel_extraction.py. The extraction process checks for duplicate years, merges available, occupied, and occupancy series by year, and calculates an independent occupancy rate:

\[ \text{Calculated occupancy} = \frac{\text{Room nights occupied}}{\text{Room nights available}} \times 100 \]

The calculated rate is retained alongside the published occupancy series rather than assumed to be equivalent to it.

Census data are assembled from the 2007, 2012, 2017, and 2022 Economic Census releases. Overlapping observations are deduplicated by year and industry. Where vintages overlap, the earlier vintage is retained in the employment analysis.

Tourism Arrivals

Visitor statistics are drawn primarily from USVIBER Annual Tourism Indicators. The analysis uses published annual totals for total visitors, air visitors, and cruise passengers. These measures are retained as reported rather than reconstructed from visitor-type components, which do not consistently sum to published totals. Island-level air and cruise arrivals for St. Thomas–St. John and St. Croix are used for intra-island comparisons only and are not assumed to reconcile with territory-wide totals.

Historical coverage combines report vintages. TOUR24 supplies 2000 and 2010–2024 observations; TOUR10 supplies 2001–2009, where the later publication does not provide a consistent historical series. The change in source vintage is documented because some historical values were revised. Accommodation-related series are retained separately where their historical coverage is consistent across publications.

The analysis does not use the overnight-tourist series. Air visitors and cruise passengers are treated as separate published arrival measures, not as proxies for overnight stays or total visitor spending. The World Bank’s 2020 international-arrivals observation is excluded as an implausible outlier during the pandemic-related shutdown of cruise tourism.

Visitor counts represent arrivals, not unique individuals. They do not measure length of stay, visitor-days, or economic contribution. Comparisons with accommodation, employment, and economic indicators therefore describe related dimensions of the tourism economy rather than a direct accounting relationship.

Employment and Compensation

Four sources provide complementary measures of employment:

  • FRED Leisure and Hospitality: Monthly seasonally adjusted and non-seasonally adjusted employment. Annual values are calculated as the mean of the twelve monthly observations, not their sum. The series covers the broader Leisure and Hospitality sector (NAICS 71 and 72), not tourism alone.
  • USVIBER tourism employment: Published employment categories for hotels and lodging, eating and drinking establishments, and other tourism-related sectors. The available series ends in 2022.
  • BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW): Annual average employment, wages, and establishments for NAICS 72, 721, and 722. QCEW counts covered jobs rather than unique workers and excludes self-employed and other uncovered workers.
  • Census Bureau, Economic Census: Periodic employment snapshots for payroll-filing businesses. Figures represent March pay period, not annual averages.

The sources differ in industry coverage, reporting population, and reference period. Census, QCEW, FRED, and USVIBER employment figures are therefore compared as indicators of related activity, not as equivalent counts.

QCEW average annual pay is calculated as total annual wages divided by annual average employment. Nominal wages and payroll are adjusted to approximate 2016 dollars using the mainland U.S. CPI-U. This is an inflation proxy, not a USVI-specific price adjustment.

Accommodation Capacity and Utilization

Accommodation capacity is measured using two distinct sources:

  • USVIBER annual tourism indicators: Survey-based counts of hotels, hotel rooms, and condominium or other accommodation units; hotel occupancy statistics.
  • Economic Census: Establishments and guestroom counts for payroll-filing accommodation businesses, primarily NAICS 7211.

These sources have different coverage. The USVIBER survey includes a broader range of accommodation providers; Census counts are limited to businesses meeting its reporting criteria. Their totals are not combined.

Historical Census guestroom counts require a crosswalk between table formats. The 2007 and 2012 figures use the A10 industry total; the 2017 and 2022 figures use NAPCS product code 7003825000. The 2017 count is a December 31 observation, after Hurricanes Irma and Maria, and is not a pre-hurricane capacity benchmark.

Occupancy is examined using both USVIBER annual indicators and monthly hotel statistics extracted from USVIBER hotel reports. Published occupancy, calculated room-night occupancy, and broader annual survey occupancy are retained as separate measures because their reporting populations and comparability have not been established as identical.

The ratio of annual overnight arrivals to listed rooms was explored as a contextual measure. It is not treated as occupancy or actual room utilization: it does not account for length of stay, room availability throughout the year, or differences in accommodation type.

Economic Output

Territorial GDP and industry value added are drawn from BEA tables:

  • Table 2.1: nominal industry value added.
  • Table 2.3: real industry value added in chained dollars.

Real-dollar comparisons use BEA’s chained-dollar measures. No additional CPI adjustment is applied.

Accommodation and food services (A&F) value added is used as an indicator of activity in a relevant part of the visitor economy, not as a measure of tourism GDP. It excludes tourism-related activity in industries such as transportation, retail, and recreation, while including purchases by residents.

A&F’s share of GDP is calculated as nominal A&F value added divided by nominal GDP. This is an approximate industry share, not a complete accounting of tourism’s contribution to the economy.

BEA industry-level data end in 2021; GDP totals extend through 2022.

Recovery Comparisons

To compare indicators measured in different units, selected series are indexed to 2016, the last full year before the 2017 hurricanes:

\[ \text{Index}_t = \frac{\text{Value}_t}{\text{Value}_{2016}} \times 100 \]

An index of 100 represents the 2016 level. Values above or below 100 indicate levels higher or lower than that baseline.

Each series is indexed independently. Series without a usable 2016 observation are excluded. Missing observations are retained as missing; they are not interpolated or treated as zero. The resulting indices facilitate comparisons of relative change, not absolute scale.

Occupancy rates are not indexed. Changes are expressed in percentage points relative to the 2016 rate, preserving the distinction between a change in a rate and a proportional change in an underlying quantity.

A separate COVID comparison uses 2019, the last pre-pandemic year, as its baseline. It examines visitor arrivals, air visitors, cruise passengers, hotel rooms, FRED employment, and VIIS visitation.

No composite recovery score is calculated. The analysis compares the trajectories of individual indicators because recovery in visitor volume, accommodation capacity, employment, compensation, and economic output need not proceed at the same rate.

National Park Visitation

National Park Service visitation data are drawn from the IRMA database for Virgin Islands National Park (VIIS) and Buck Island Reef National Monument (BUIS). Annual and monthly observations are retained separately.

NPS visitation represents estimated recreation visits, not unique visitors. VIIS estimates also involve changes in counting methods over time. The 2003–2021 and 2022–2025 estimates are treated as distinct methodological periods; 2026 observations are excluded from long-term comparisons because of a major change to traffic-counter-based estimation. Missing BUIS observations for 1999–2001 are not interpolated.

Data Quality and Limitations

Source discrepancies and known data-quality issues are documented in src/validation.py. The project maintains a structured data-quality log recording the issue, severity, treatment, and implications for interpretation.

Key decisions include:

  • Excluding the anomalous 2020 World Bank international-arrivals observation rather than treating it as valid.
  • Using published USVIBER visitor totals and categories directly rather than reconstructing totals from components that do not consistently reconcile.
  • Combining historical USVIBER tourism-indicator vintages where necessary, while documenting material differences between publications.
  • Retaining gaps in NPS and hotel reporting rather than interpolating missing years.
  • Treating 2025 hotel observations as partial where the source reporting period does not cover a full year.
  • Using FRED employment after the USVIBER employment series ends in 2022.
  • Treating mainland CPI-U as an approximate deflator for USVI wages, not a local cost-of-living measure.

The analysis is constrained by differences in source coverage, definitions, reporting frequency, and revision history. Indexed comparisons reduce differences in scale but do not eliminate these limitations. Conclusions are therefore based on converging or diverging indicators, not on any single series.

Reproducibility

The project separates acquisition, validation, cleaning, hotel extraction, and chart construction into modules under src/. Raw source files are retained separately from processed datasets. Processed hotel statistics are generated from the curated extraction files; the analysis loads the resulting annual dataset.

The analysis scripts and source registry document the inputs and transformations used to produce the figures and comparisons. Data-quality decisions are recorded in the validation log.
